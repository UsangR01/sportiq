# Top 30 Non-Mainstream Football Leagues for Data-Driven Betting Prediction

## Objective

This ranking focuses on less-mainstream football leagues that may be attractive for a data-driven football prediction model.

The leagues are not ranked purely by betting volume. The ranking considers four broad factors:

- Betting-market availability and liquidity
- Historical data availability
- Statistical stability of outcomes
- Potential for modelling BTTS, Over/Under 2.5 and 1X2

> **Important:** A league having a high historical BTTS or Over 2.5 rate does not automatically mean that betting those markets is profitable. Bookmakers generally incorporate historical patterns into their prices.

## The Key Principle: Prediction vs Betting Value

A major mistake when building a football betting model is assuming that a league's historical statistics directly translate into profitable bets.

For example:

> League BTTS rate = **65%**

This does not mean you should automatically bet BTTS whenever the historical rate is 65%.

The model should instead estimate the probability for the **specific match** and compare it with the bookmaker's implied probability.

### Example

Suppose:

- Model predicted BTTS probability = **72%**
- Bookmaker BTTS odds = **1.70**
- Implied probability = approximately **58.8%**
- Model edge = approximately **13.2 percentage points**

The difference between the model probability and the bookmaker's implied probability is much more informative than the league-wide BTTS rate.

## Recommended Model Architecture

For an AI/ML betting system, the strongest approach would not be to simply predict whether a team will win.

A better architecture would be:

```text
Historical Match Data
        ↓
Feature Engineering
        ↓
Team Strength / Form Model
        ↓
Expected Goals Model
        ↓
Match Probability Model
        ↓
BTTS / O2.5 / U2.5 / 1X2 Probabilities
        ↓
Compare Against Bookmaker Odds
        ↓
Calculate Expected Value
        ↓
Liquidity / Confidence Filters
        ↓
Prediction / Bet Selection
```

## The Most Important Filter: Expected Value

The system should ultimately focus on **value**, rather than simply predicting winners.

A simplified framework is:

```text
Model Probability = P

Bookmaker Decimal Odds = O

Implied Probability = 1 / O

Expected Value = (P × O) - 1
```

For example:

```text
Model probability = 0.72
Odds = 1.70

EV = (0.72 × 1.70) - 1
EV = 0.224
EV = +22.4%
```

This would indicate positive expected value under the model's assumptions.

## Liquidity Matters

A league can look statistically attractive while being unsuitable for an automated betting strategy if the available market is too thin.

Liquidity should therefore be treated as a filter.

A practical system could classify markets as:

| Liquidity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Very high | Automatically eligible |
| High | Eligible |
| Medium | Eligible with higher confidence threshold |
| Low | Manual review |
| Very low | Exclude |

This is particularly important for smaller leagues where bookmaker prices may move sharply and available stakes may be limited.

## Important Caveat

Historical league-level percentages are descriptive statistics, not guarantees of future results.

A model should be tested using:

- Walk-forward validation
- Out-of-sample seasons
- Calibration testing
- Closing Line Value (CLV)
- ROI
- Maximum drawdown
- Brier score
- Log loss
- Precision/recall where appropriate
- Bankroll simulations

Most importantly, avoid evaluating a betting model purely on whether its picks "win". A model can have a high win rate and still lose money if the odds are too short.
